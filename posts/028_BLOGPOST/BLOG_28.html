Goal

My goal here was to have Gemini help me create a marathon training plan around my schedule as a student athlete here at Penn.

This was my original Prompt:

help me to plan a marathon training schedule. I have class everyweek day from 1:45-6 and Lacrosse practice in the mornings from 8:30-noon. I am aiming to do the marathon in may of 2026

### And here is what Gemini returned me

That’s an ambitious goal! Training for a marathon while managing a busy school and practice schedule requires a very disciplined and strategic approach. Since the marathon is in May 2026, you have about 17 months to prepare, which is a fantastic amount of time.

Here is a breakdown of how we can structure your training and a draft schedule that works around your commitments.

🏃 Marathon Training Strategy

Your schedule is quite demanding, meaning we need to prioritize:

  1. Long Runs: These are the most critical runs and should happen on the weekend when you have the most free time.
  2. Recovery/Cross-Training: Given your intense lacrosse practice, we need to schedule dedicated recovery time. Lacrosse is a high-intensity sport and will likely serve as some of your cross-training, but we must be careful not to overtrain or cause injury.
  3. Consistency: The key to marathon training is slowly increasing mileage over many months to avoid injury.

📅 Phased Training Plan (Starting January 2025)

Since you have 17 months, we can divide the training into four main phases. This provides a slow, safe, and sustainable buildup.

1. Base Building Phase (January – May 2025: 5 months)

The goal here is to establish a solid running foundation and make running a consistent habit without burning out.

  • Focus: Easy, comfortable runs, focusing on consistency and form.
  • Weekly Mileage: Start low (e.g., 10-15 miles per week) and increase by no more than \(10\%\) per week.
  • Key Runs:
    • 3-4 Easy Runs (30-45 minutes) on your flexible weeknight slot (6:00 PM).
    • 1 Long Run on Sunday (Start at 3 miles and increase by 1 mile every 1-2 weeks).

2. Strength & Endurance Phase (June – September 2025: 4 months)

Once your body is accustomed to running 4-5 times a week, we can introduce more challenging workouts.

  • Focus: Increasing weekly mileage and adding intensity to build speed and stamina.
  • Weekly Mileage: Continue the gradual increase, aiming to comfortably reach 25-30 miles per week.
  • Key Runs:
    • Easy Runs: Maintain 3-4 easy runs.
    • Tempo/Speed Run: Replace one easy run with a challenging session (e.g., Tuesday at 6:00 PM). This could be a tempo run (sustained hard effort) or an interval session.
    • Long Run: Continue increasing the Sunday run, aiming to reach 8-10 miles comfortably by the end of this phase.

3. Peak Training Phase (October 2025 – February 2026: 5 months)

This is the core of your marathon training, where the biggest mileage is logged.

  • Focus: Building the necessary endurance to complete the marathon distance.
  • Weekly Mileage: This is where you’ll hit your peak (up to 40-55 miles, depending on your goal). Crucially, incorporate “down weeks” (lower mileage) every 3-4 weeks to allow your body to recover.
  • Key Runs:
    • Long Run: The Sunday run should progress to 12, 14, 16, 18, and eventually 20 miles. Only run 20 miles once or twice.
    • Mid-Week Medium Run: A slightly longer run (6-8 miles) on a Wednesday or Thursday.
    • Quality Run: Continue the Tuesday tempo/speed work.

4. Taper Phase (March – May 2026: 2 months)

This is the final countdown before the race.

  • Focus: Resting your legs and storing energy. The hard work is done!
  • Weekly Mileage: Significantly reduce mileage (e.g., \(75\%\) of peak, then \(50\%\), then \(25\%\)).
  • Key Runs: Runs are much shorter, focusing on feeling refreshed. Keep the Tuesday speed session short and fast to maintain muscle memory, but stop the very long runs. The final week is mostly rest.

🔑 Crucial Notes for Your Unique Schedule

  • Lacrosse as Cross-Training: Your lacrosse practice is high-impact cardio. You must be extremely diligent about listening to your body. If your legs are sore from practice, make your planned run an easier one, or take a rest day. Prioritize injury prevention above all else.
  • Nutrition and Sleep: Given your high-volume activity (school, lacrosse, and running), nutrition, hydration, and sleep must be your top priority. You cannot sustain this schedule without proper fueling.
  • Flex Day (Saturday): Use Saturday morning for a no-impact cross-training session (swimming, cycling, elliptical) to keep your fitness up without the stress of , or for a very short, easy shakeout run to loosen your legs for the Sunday long run.

Analysis:

I was a little underwhelmed by the outcome. First off, Gemini told me I had 17 months to prepare, when in fact I have 5.5, which is clearly a delusional response from the LLM. Secondly, the model provided very little explanation of why each training session is what it is. The model just spit out my schedule to me and provided a brief explanation of what I should be doing in my limited free time. I was unhappy with the response because I know this goal is a bit lofty given my packed schedule but this is precisely why I chase to do this. I wanted to see the model get creative and come up with some sort of nuance that maximizes the limited free time I do have. I would also have liked more questions about my body type and running experience, rather than a one-size-fits-all attempt to please me with a quick response.
